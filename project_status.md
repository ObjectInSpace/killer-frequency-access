# Killer Frequency Access: project status

Last updated: 2026-11-23

## State

Unreleased. Loads in MelonLoader, speaks through Prism (v0.18.4, `native\win-x64\prism.dll`, next to the mod in Mods) via the
Prism.Net bindings (`third_party\Prism.Net`, compiled into the mod), reads menus. A screen reader started
or stopped mid-game is picked up from Prism's availability polling. Targeting has been rebuilt on
the game's own types (2026-11-04): all name, collider, interaction and handler guessing is gone
(`WorldNavigation.cs` went from about 4,800 lines to about 2,100). The installed DLL is that build;
it has not been run yet.

**Rule:** no guesswork. Everything the mod says comes from the game's typed API at runtime or from
data generated from the rip. Where a fact is unknown, the mod logs it instead of guessing.

## How targeting works now

`KillerFrequencyAccessMod.Interactables.cs`:
- Discovery: `FindObjectsOfType<Il2Cpp.InteractableBase>()`; listed when active, not `OculusOnly`,
  and at least one of its `Il2CppActions.InteractionBase` components reports `Available`.
- Actions: the interaction's Il2Cpp class name through a fixed verb table (every class in the rip),
  ordered by `Priority`. An unknown class is spoken by class name and logged.
- Names: `Generated\GameObjectNames.cs`, generated by `tools\rip\gen_names.py` from the rip, keyed
  by full hierarchy path; prefab name plus a role from its components (door, handle, button,
  holder, board item). Fallback: a leaf name that maps to one name only; else the raw name, logged
  as `[names] not in GameObjectNames`. Review list: `docs\rip\spoken_names.txt`.
- Player: the `ControllerBaseInteractor` (on `PlayerController`, which also has the
  `CharacterController`; one per scene). It is the navigation origin.
- Pointing at: `ControllerBaseInteractor.observedInteractable`. Auto-walk stops when the game reports
  the target as observed; the mod has no reach estimate of its own.

`KillerFrequencyAccessMod.ObjectList.cs` (object list, user design 2026-11-05, always on outside menus):
- A tree: rooms, then what stands on its own in the room (devices, drawers, doors, docks, loose
  objects), then for every container at any depth its direct contents (an object's parent is its
  nearest Interactable / Openable / device ancestor). End goes in, Home comes out, Page Up / Page Down
  move along a level; Backspace walks to the entry (a device's first usable part). The list follows
  the player: entering a room lists its objects, looking at an object selects it, looking at a
  container lists its contents. O, Enter, `[` / `]` and categories are
  gone. Keys are ones the game's `InputActions` asset leaves unbound (the arrows are movement).
- Rooms: the game's 25 `Il2Cpp.Room`s. `Room.volumes` is empty at runtime, so a room's space is
  the Unity `OcclusionArea` boxes on its children (offline check `tools\rip\room_check.py`: 797 of
  804 SCN_Main objects fall in a room). Name = floor folder + room object ("Ground Floor Break Room").
  The first room entry is the player's room and follows them. Entering a room is announced.
- Doors: `Il2Cpp.RoomPortalDoor` (_door, _frontRoom, _backRoom, isOpen) lists the 16 room doors in
  both rooms they join, announced as "door, to <room>". Before, one point decided an object's room,
  and the studio door's pivot lies 0.6 m inside the Green Room box (rip), so only the Green Room had it.
  The other 43 DoorOpenables belong to cabinets, the fridge, the safe, sinks, stalls and dumpsters.
- Containment: the outermost ancestor that is an Interactable, an `Openable` or a device component
  (VinylPlayer, CassettePlayer, Dictaphone, SoundBoard, FuseBox, NumericKeypad, HandheldRadio,
  FloppyDiskComputer, Lever, Board). The studio's phone-line buttons sit on `P_HeroProp_ConsoleMixer_05`,
  which has no component, so they are their own entries next to the soundboard (a part of that console).
- Hidden: a room's contents while the player is elsewhere and all its doorways are closed (its doors
  stay; a plain `RoomPortal` is always open), and anything below a closed `Openable`. Loose items in
  cabinets and the fridge are not children of them (rip: no slots), so they are still listed.
- Actions spoken for the object you point at use `CanPerform(interactor)` (first run: `Available`
  was true for drop and throw on items not held), each with its keys read live from the game's
  `InputActions` asset ("pick up, left click or numpad 0"). Interactions with no
  `controllerInputs` are VR only (every Grasp/Release, 93 of 107 Dock) and are not listed or spoken.
- Distances: one decimal under 3 m, plus "above" / "below" beyond 0.3 m from camera height.

`KillerFrequencyAccessMod.HeldItems.cs`:
- Announces what you hold (`GetPrimaryHeldInteractable` / `GetSecondaryHeldInteractable`) and
  where it fits: every `PhysicsHoldableDock` whose `CanDock(held)` is true (the game's check, which
  includes callbacks such as `VinylPlayer.OnCanDockVinylCallback`). In flat-screen mode an item goes
  into a dock with Place (right click or numpad 2) while aiming at it. Object-list category
  "places for what you hold"; pointing at a fitting dock says "Fits X, place with ...".
- Logs `[held]`, `[performing]` (the interactor's `PerformingInteractions`), `[keys]`.

Names: parts of a device are named after it (`tools\rip\gen_names.py`, `DEVICES`): a holder, button
or slider below `VinylPlayer` is "record player ..." ("record player vinyl slot"), likewise cassette
player, dictaphone, soundboard, fuse box, keypad, handheld radio, computer, lever.

The environment scan (the \ key, raycast open-path tones) was removed on 2026-11-04 at the user's
request: it never worked and wasn't useful. Unity audio is disabled in this game (`m_DisableAudio: 1`),
so mod sounds go through winmm.

## How auto-walk works now (2026-11-23)

Backspace walks to the selected object. The walk keeps its own NavMesh routing and follows Date
Everything Access's shape above it (`SimpleNavBridge`, `SimpleNavCoverageSweep`). The facts behind every
number are in `docs\game-api.md` ("Player body", "Walkable area", "Doors, containers and placing").
- Route (`NavGraph.cs`, data `Generated\Nav\*.navbin.gz` from `tools\rip\gen_navmesh.py`): the scene's
  own NavMesh polygons from the rip, picked by scene name (the game will not be updated). A* over
  polygons, then the funnel run one 0.3 m height band at a time, so routes between floors go by the
  stairs and never through a floor. Both ends are snapped onto one connected region. Nothing else is
  used: the 0.5 m lattice over `NavMesh.SamplePosition` could not climb the stairs and was removed.
- Where to stand (`FindStandpoints` in `AutoWalk.cs`): points around the target on every floor under
  them, at both eye heights (1.6 standing, 0.9 crouched), within `fsMaxReachDistance`, inside the
  45 degree pitch limit, with the game's interaction ray hitting the target first. Nearest first,
  crouched spots counted 0.5 m further. A spot with no route is skipped.
- Posture (`WalkPosture.cs`): stand while more than 1.5 m of route remain (faster), then take the
  spot's posture through `ConfigureForMoveMode`; standing checks headroom on `crouchCheckLayerMask`.
  A posture change by the player ends this for the walk.
- Doors (`WalkDoors.cs`): a closed room door whose portal the route crosses is opened 1.5 m before it
  (`Openable.Open()`); a locked one stops the walk. Unmoved after 3 s: the walk stops and says so.
- Hidden targets (`WalkContainers.cs`): with no spot, the colliders the sight lines hit say why. A
  closed container is walked to and opened, then the walk goes on; a holder (a tape in a slot) becomes
  the target.
- Obstacles (`WalkObstacles.cs`): on a stall the player's capsule is cast toward the next corner; the
  collider hit gets a footprint, and a detour around it over the graph rejoins the route. None: "Blocked
  by X. No way around".
- Corners count as reached within 0.3 m, and 0.6 m of height (stairs). The view turns through
  `CameraHandler.ResetToRotation`. Arrival = the game observes the target (whatever its actions), or,
  for a dock that fits the held item, the view is on it (docks are never observed while holding).
- Coverage sweep (`CoverageSweep.cs`, F8): walks to every listed object, nearest first, twice at most,
  no teleporting. Results: `UserData\KillerFrequencyAccess\sweeps\<run>\results.jsonl` and `summary.txt`.
- Logs: `[walk]` (spots, route, posture, doors, containers, stalls, detours, stop reason), `[nav]`,
  `[place]`, `[sweep]`.

## Findings (2026-11-04)

- The game is a radio station the player walks around in first person, with desk scopes (mixer,
  pinboard, record drawer). See `docs\game-api.md`.
- The game has two interaction systems in code. The scenes use Radio.Actions
  (`Il2CppActions.Interactables.Interactable` with `Il2CppInteractables.*` actions).
  `Il2Cpp.InteractableItem` and the `RootScope`/`DeskChairScope`/... scopes are never placed in any
  scene or prefab.
- SCN_Main has 804 interactables; SCN_Intro 51, SCN_Epilogue 46, SCN_Menu 6. Lists:
  `docs\rip\interactables_*.txt`.
- Every `interactionScope` field in the scenes is null; scope must come from the runtime.
- The game names no objects anywhere: the reticle is an icon, and the localization tables in the rip
  are UI text only.
- NavMesh: SCN_Main uses `NavMesh_0.asset`, Intro and Menu `NavMesh_1.asset`, Epilogue `NavMesh.asset`.
- SCN_Main has 25 `Room`s, 17 `RoomPortalDoor`s, 59 `DoorOpenable`s.

## Boards and maps (investigation 2026-11-23, offline)

The visual content is in textures. The AssetRipper rip has none (`Texture2D` is empty), so
`tools\rip\extract_textures.py` reads them from the game with UnityPy. `tools\rip\dump_strings.py` dumps
all 7,146 English strings (`Collection:Key`) from the localization bundles; the dialogue is there.
`DiegeticTextLocalizer._displayText` is not serialised in the rip (374 components, all empty), so label
text exists only at runtime (`LocalizedStringToDisplay`).

- **Boards** (`Il2Cpp.Board`, three in SCN_Main): a plane with x/y limits, `_docks`, `_boardItems`.
  `BoardItem` has `_fact` (a FactAsset; `Board.UpdateAvailableNotes` shows items as facts become known),
  `_movableDock` (player drags it around the board) and `ItemDock`. No strings link items; the player's
  arrangement is free.
  - Alibi board, two copies (Janitor's office in the basement, `..._WheeledDJRoom` in the main studio):
    14 movable clippings, 1 fixed class-of-69 yearbook page, 8 fixed post-its (1 Chuck Brody, 2 Kim
    Walker, 3 Rebecca Allen, 4 Ant Williams; Gas Station, Power Station, Hospital, Trailer Park). Each item
    is a patch of atlas `T_AlibiBoard_Final_01` / `_02` (mesh UVs). The puzzle is answered in dialogue
    (`Alibi:AL_Choice_Name_*`, `AL_Choice_Location_*`), so the player needs each item's content: headlines,
    dates, article text, and the picture (e.g. the yearbook circles Kim Walker, Rebecca Allen, Ant Williams
    and Chuck Brody in red; one polaroid is a bomb/pipe photo).
  - Gallows Creek map board (`P_HeroProp_GallowsCreekMap`): 89 `DiegeticTextLocalizer` labels (places and
    streets, positioned on the map), movable markers A-E and pin tacks 1-5, fixed location-hint cards
    (Alex: Haddonfield Road at Romero Street; Katherine: west end of Myers Lane; Old Man Jericho: east end
    of Myers Lane; Fire Department) and a traffic notice (east Macready Street closed between Rogers
    Avenue and Haddonfield Road). Map texture `T_GallowsCreekMap_AlbedoTransparency`; a second copy is a
    poster.
- **Maize maze map** (`P_HeroProp_Interactable_MaizeMaze`, texture `T_MaizeMaze_AlbedoTransparency`):
